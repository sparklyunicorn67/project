Fire & Emergency Services

Emergency response across the county is coordinated through Cumberland County Emergency Services.

Fire & Rescue

The county's Emergency Communications Center dispatches for all county fire departments and coordinates responses to fire/rescue incidents.

Photo: Fayetteville Fire Station 14, 632 Langdon St., Fayetteville. Image by Stewart Cooper Newell Architects.

EMS

Cape Fear Valley EMS provides county-wide emergency medical service and is dispatched through the county communications center.

Photo: Cape Fear Valley Medical Center, 1638 Owen Drive, Fayetteville.

911

For an immediate threat to life or property, fire, medical emergency, serious accident, or crime in progress, call 911. Text-to-911 is also available in appropriate situations.

Public Schools in Fayetteville

Fayetteville is the county seat and largest city, and most public schools in the city are part of Cumberland County Schools.

The district serves students throughout Cumberland County, including Fayetteville, Hope Mills, Spring Lake, and surrounding communities.

Public Transportation

Fayetteville and Cumberland County are connected by buses, air travel, passenger rail, and major highways.

FAST

The Fayetteville Area System of Transit (FAST) operates fixed-route bus service in Fayetteville, with 18 routes and 33 fixed-route buses listed by the city.

Photo: FAST bus, City of Fayetteville.

FASTTrac!

FASTTrac! is an ADA paratransit service for eligible riders who cannot use the fixed-route system because of a qualifying disability. The FAST Transit Center is a central hub for the local bus network.

Photo: FAST Transit Center, 505 Franklin Street, Fayetteville.

Other Connections

History of the Area

Cumberland County's story is tied to the Cape Fear River, Fayetteville, and military history.

1729–1736
Highland Scots and other European migrants settled in the Upper Cape Fear Valley.
1754
Cumberland County was formed from part of Bladen County and named for William Augustus, Duke of Cumberland.
1783
Campbellton was renamed Fayetteville in honor of the Marquis de Lafayette.
1831
A devastating fire destroyed much of Fayetteville, damaging more than 600 buildings.
1865
General Sherman's army reached Fayetteville during the Civil War, and the Fayetteville Arsenal was destroyed.
1918–present
The area became closely connected to the U.S. Army through Camp Bragg, later renamed Fort Bragg, then Fort Liberty in 2023 and back to Fort Bragg in 2025.

Visit the Airborne & Special Operations Museum, Fayetteville History Museum, Museum of the Cape Fear, and 1897 Poe House.

Get outdoors

Military heritage

Explore the area's military museums, memorials, and public spaces connected to generations of service members and families.

Common Jobs & Major Employment Areas

Cumberland County's economy reflects its military population and its role as a regional service center.

Healthcare & Social Assistance

Hospitals, clinics, nursing, medical support, and social-service organizations are major parts of the local workforce.

Education

Public schools, colleges, and education support services employ thousands of people across the county.

Retail & Food Service

Retail stores, restaurants, hospitality, and customer-service jobs are common in the Fayetteville regional market.

Manufacturing

Manufacturing remains an important local sector, including industrial and advanced-manufacturing opportunities.

Construction & Trades

Construction, skilled trades, repair, and related work support residential and commercial growth.

Transportation & Logistics

I-95, the airport, rail, retail activity, and military operations create demand for transportation, warehousing, and logistics workers.

Military & Defense

Fort Bragg is a major economic and community presence, supporting military occupations as well as civilian jobs connected to defense and services.

Information Technology

Local workforce-development plans identify information technology and cybersecurity-related skills as important growth areas.

Public Service

County and municipal government employ people in public safety, administration, health, parks, inspections, and many other fields.

Employment sectors: Cumberland County Consolidated Plan and Cumberland County Workforce Development.
